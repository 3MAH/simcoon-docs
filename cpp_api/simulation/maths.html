Mathematical Functions

Mathematical utility functions for rotations, random number generation, statistics, and numerical solving.

Overview

The Maths module provides essential mathematical utilities for continuum mechanics simulations:

  • Rotation: the Rotation class and the Voigt rotation helpers (see The Rotation Module)

  • Random numbers and statistics: random draws, probability distributions, peak functions

  • Local solvers: Newton and Fischer-Burmeister solvers of the return-mapping systems, Lagrange penalty functions, quadratic roots

  • Fixed-size kernels: 3x3 spectral decompositions and fixed-order products used in the per-point loops

bool eig_sym_3x3(arma::vec::fixed<3> &lam, arma::mat::fixed<3, 3> &Q, const arma::mat::fixed<3, 3> &A)

Eigen-decomposition of a symmetric 3x3 matrix, \( \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{Q}\,\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\,\mathbf{Q}^T \).

Fixed-size storage, no heap allocation and no LAPACK call. Two solvers behind one contract:

  • the deviatoric closed form of Scherzinger and Dohrmann (2008): the most distinct eigenvalue \( \eta_1 \) of \( \mathbf{S} = \mathrm{dev}\,\mathbf{A} \) from the Lode angle, its eigenvector from two columns of \( \mathbf{S} - \eta_1\mathbf{I} \), then one rotation of the 2x2 restriction on the complement. Robust for double and nearly double eigenvalues, accurate to \( \epsilon\,\max_a|\lambda_a| \), about 2.4 times cheaper than Jacobi;

  • cyclic Jacobi rotations (Kopp 2008, dsyevj3), accurate to the last digits for every eigenvalue, the small ones of a stiff SPD matrix included. It serves the matrices with at most one nonzero off-diagonal entry (a diagonal A is returned exactly, Q a permutation), and replaces the closed form when \( \min_a|\lambda_a| < 10^{-2}\max_a|\lambda_a| \), where the absolute accuracy of the closed form is no longer a relative one on the small eigenvalue.

Only the upper triangle of A is read. The eigenvalues come in ascending order, as with arma::eig_sym; the eigenvectors are the orthonormal columns of Q (sign arbitrary).

This is the solver behind the per-point spectral functions of the finite-strain kinematics (polar decompositions, logarithmic strains, Daleckii-Krein derivatives).

Parameters:

  • lam – [out] eigenvalues, ascending

  • Q – [out] eigenvectors (columns)

  • A – [in] symmetric matrix (upper triangle read)

Returns:

false when A is not finite or the iteration does not converge (as arma::eig_sym)

void Fischer_Burmeister_m(const arma::vec&, const arma::vec&, const arma::mat&, arma::vec&, arma::vec&, double&)

One Newton step of the normalised Fischer-Burmeister reformulation of the local Kuhn-Tucker conditions (the default complementarity function of the cutting planes).

The return mapping seeks multipliers \( \Delta p_i \) with \( \Phi_i \le 0,\ \Delta p_i \ge 0,\ \Phi_i\,\Delta p_i = 0 \) for each surface \( i \). With the normalised multiplier \( \Delta p_i^* = \Delta p_i\,|B_{ii}| \) (a stress, as \( \Phi_i \)) these three conditions are the single equation

\[ \phi_i = \sqrt{\Phi_i^2 + \Delta p_i^{*2}} + \Phi_i - \Delta p_i^* = 0, \]
the Fischer-Burmeister function \( \sqrt{a^2+b^2} - a - b \) with \( a = -\Phi_i \), \( b = \Delta p_i^* \). It is smooth away from \( (0, 0) \) and decides by itself whether a surface is active, so the same equation serves one surface or a coupled system of N. The Newton step solves \( J\,\delta p = -\phi \) with, \( r_i = \sqrt{\Phi_i^2 + \Delta p_i^{*2}} \),
\[ J_{ij} = \left(\frac{\Phi_i}{r_i} + 1\right) B_{ij} + \delta_{ij}\,|B_{ii}| \left(\frac{\Delta p_i^*}{r_i} - 1\right), \qquad B_{ij} = \frac{\partial \Phi_i}{\partial \Delta p_j}. \]
From \( \Delta p = 0 \) on a violated surface ( \( \Phi > 0 \), \( B < 0 \)) one row gives \( \phi = 2\Phi \), \( J = 3B \): the step is two thirds of the full cutting-plane step \( -\Phi/B \), which ncp_step with NCPMethod::min_fb_fallback takes instead. A row with \( \Phi_i = \Delta p_i^* = 0 \) is frozen ( \( J_{ii} = 10^{12} \)); a singular \( J \) gives a zero step. One and two surfaces are solved in closed form, more by LAPACK.

Parameters:

  • Phi – [in] constraints \( \Phi_i \) (N)

  • Y_crit – [in] normalisations of the error (N, non-zero), typically the yield stresses

  • denom – [in] local Jacobian \( B = \partial \Phi / \partial \Delta p \) (N x N)

  • Dp – [inout] multipliers, updated: \( \Delta p \leftarrow \Delta p + \delta p \)

  • dp – [out] the step \( \delta p \)

  • error – [out] \( \sum_i |\phi_i| / |Y^{crit}_i| \) of the entering iterate (Fischer_Burmeister_residual)

Throws:

simcoon::exception_det – when LAPACK fails on a system of more than two surfaces

double Fischer_Burmeister_residual(const arma::vec &Phi, const arma::vec &Y_crit, const arma::mat &denom, const arma::vec &Dp)

The convergence measure of Fischer_Burmeister_m without its Newton step.

\( \sum_i |\phi_i| / |Y^{crit}_i| \) with the normalised Fischer-Burmeister function \( \phi_i = \sqrt{\Phi_i^2 + \Delta p_i^{*2}} + \Phi_i - \Delta p_i^* \), \( \Delta p_i^* = \Delta p_i\,|B_{ii}| \) — the very expression (branches included) Fischer_Burmeister_m returns in its error argument for the entering iterate, so a line search (closest_point_return_mapping) can rate a trial point without solving the \( N \times N \) system. Kept in step with Fischer_Burmeister_m by construction: that function’s error loop is this one.

Parameters:

  • Phi – [in] constraints (N)

  • Y_crit – [in] normalisations (N, non-zero)

  • denom – [in] local Jacobian (N x N); only |diag| is used

  • Dp – [in] multipliers (N)

Returns:

the residual

class ncp_step
#include <num_solve.hpp>

One Newton step per call of a local complementarity solve, with the chosen function.

NCPMethod::fischer_burmeister forwards to Fischer_Burmeister_m. NCPMethod::min_fb_fallback takes the semi-smooth Newton step of \( \min(\Delta p^*_i, -\Phi_i) = 0 \) ( \( \Delta p^*_i = \Delta p_i |B_{ii}| \)): on an active row ( \( \Phi_i > 0 \) or \( -\Phi_i < \Delta p^*_i \)) the linearised constraint \( \Phi_i + B_{i\cdot}\,\delta p = 0 \), the classic cutting plane (Ortiz and Simo 1986), on an inactive one the multiplier back to zero (primal-dual active set, Hintermueller, Ito and Kunisch 2002). The first step from \( \Delta p = 0 \) is the full Newton step, where the Fischer-Burmeister function only takes two thirds of it: a linear hardening returns in one step instead of six. Its error is \( \sum_i 2|r_i|/|Y^{crit}_i| \) of the entering iterate, the scale of the Fischer-Burmeister measure near the solution. It applies row by row when the rows are uncoupled (denom diagonal): a single surface, or a surface next to the always-satisfied rows of the viscoelastic and damage mechanisms of MODUL ( \( \Phi = -Y^{crit} \), no multiplier, no cross term), which stay at zero. A coupled system (off-diagonal terms, e.g. several yield surfaces) keeps the Fischer-Burmeister step under min_fb_fallback, the active set of coupled surfaces making the plain semi-smooth Newton cycle. When the error grows from one call to the next, or a row is singular, the solve falls back to Fischer-Burmeister for the remaining calls (a nonlinear kinematic hardening can make the full step overshoot).

NCPMethod::active_set is the min step on every row: the active rows ( \( \Phi_i > 0 \) or \( -\Phi_i < \Delta p^*_i \)) solve their linearised constraints together, \( \Phi_A + B_{AA}\,\delta p_A + B_{AI}\,\delta p_I = 0 \) with the inactive multipliers sent back to zero ( \( \delta p_I = -\Delta p_I \)); on uncoupled rows it is the min_fb_fallback step, step for step, on coupled ones the primal-dual active set of Hintermueller, Ito and Kunisch, with the same fallback to Fischer-Burmeister (error growth, singular system) as its combinatorial safeguard.

Construct one object per local solve: it holds the fallback state of that solve.

class frame_rotation
#include <rotation.hpp>

A rotation with its Voigt operators materialized once, applied in place.

Rotation::apply_* rebuilds the \( 3\times3 \) matrix and the \( 6\times6 \) Voigt operators at every call. When one rotation is applied many times (a material frame at every UMAT call, see material_characteristics::frame()), this class stores the operators of the rotation and of its inverse once and applies them with a single fixed-size product each, without heap temporary. The results are those of Rotation::apply_* on the rotation (inverse = false) or on Rotation::inv() (inverse = true) to the bit: the same operator matrices are built, once instead of per call, and Armadillo’s fixed-size products of this size evaluate as the dynamic ones. On the identity rotation (Rotation::is_identity) every apply is a no-op, as the skipped product would be.

Immutable after construction, hence safe to read from several threads.
