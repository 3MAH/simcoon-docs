Building materials in Python (modular)

The simcoon.modular package is the Python builder for the composable MODUL engine: a constitutive model is assembled from an elasticity definition plus any combination of mechanisms (plasticity, viscoelasticity, damage), and serialized into the self-describing props stream that the C++ engine consumes. The same object drives the in-memory solver, the file solver, or any FEA coupling — see the UMAT catalog for how MODUL fits among the other material names. Units are MPa; Voigt order is [11, 22, 33, 12, 13, 23].

Quick start

from simcoon.modular import (
    ModularMaterial, IsotropicElasticity,
    Plasticity, VonMisesYield, VoceHardening, ArmstrongFrederickHardening,
)
from simcoon import solver

mat = ModularMaterial(
    elasticity=IsotropicElasticity(C1=210000.0, C2=0.3, alpha=1.2e-5,
                                   convention="Enu"),
    mechanisms=[
        Plasticity(
            sigma_Y=300.0,
            yield_criterion=VonMisesYield(),
            isotropic_hardening=VoceHardening(Q=200.0, b=10.0),
            kinematic_hardening=ArmstrongFrederickHardening(C=20000.0, D=100.0),
        ),
    ],
)
print(mat.summary())

step = solver.StepMeca(control=['strain'] + ['stress'] * 5,
                       value=[0.02, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0], ninc=100)
res = solver.solve(step, "MODUL", mat.props, mat.nstatev)

mat.props and mat.nstatev are all any caller needs — they work identically with simcoon.umat() point evaluation, mean-field micromechanics (whose phases are passed in memory through the phases argument of solve()) and FEA couplings such as fedoo.

alpha are the thermal-expansion coefficients (per direction where applicable).

The hyperelastic blocks are not a constant stiffness: the potential is evaluated at the elastic strain it is handed, bridged by \(\mathbf{b}^{el} = \exp(2\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{el})\). Under finite strain that strain is the elastic logarithmic strain, and the mechanisms act additively on it. Like every other law, the block defines its stored energy per reference volume as a function of the elastic strain, \(\Psi(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{el}) = W(\mathbf{b}^{el}) + U(J^{el})\), and returns the Kirchhoff stress of \(W\) at \(\mathbf{F} := \mathbf{V}^{el}\). It equals \(\partial \Psi / \partial \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{el}\) for an isotropic potential, and for the anisotropic HolzapfelElasticity only while it stays coaxial with \(\mathbf{V}^{el}\); the Stress output is \(\boldsymbol{\tau}/J\) with the total \(J\). The volumetric term only sees the elastic volume change \(J^{el} = \exp(\mathrm{tr}\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{el})\), so a free thermal expansion stays stress-free. Every potential shares its volumetric term \(U(J)\), chosen by the volumetric keyword: "log" (default) for \(U = \kappa (J \ln J - J + 1)\), "quadratic" for \(U = \frac{\kappa}{2} (J - 1)^2\). Both have \(U''(1) = \kappa\), so kappa is the ground-state bulk modulus either way; they differ away from \(J = 1\) (the quadratic one gives a pressure linear in \(J - 1\), the logarithmic one stiffens in compression and blows up as \(J \to 0\)).

Isotropic hardening

The isotropic hardening law \(R(p)\) of the accumulated plastic strain \(p\). \(R\) is the hardening stress, the growth of the yield stress (\(\sigma_Y + R(p)\), \(R(0) = 0\)); in the notation of the theory manual it is \(F_p = \partial F/\partial p\), the derivative of the hardening function of the free energy. Its work \(\int R\,\mathrm{d}p\) is the irrecoverable work Wm_ir:

Tangent operator and finite strain

MODUL honors the solver’s tangent_mode (continuum or algorithmic, algorithmic being the 2.0 default — Use the solver). The algorithmic tangent of a composition is the exact derivative of its discrete update whenever the plasticity it contains is exact on its own (von Mises with isotropic hardening): the viscoelastic branches take a closed-form step and enter by the chain rule, and damage scales the effective response (plasticity yields on the effective stress \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}/(1-D)\)). With kinematic hardening or another yield criterion the plastic cutting-plane update makes it approximate (see the note on the scope of the algorithmic tangent in Use the solver). Under the finite-strain control types the composition acts as a Hencky hyperelastic law on the logarithmic strain and requires corate_type = 3 (log_R) — which is the simcoon.solver.solve() default, so nothing needs to be passed. Any other corate (e.g. corate="logarithmic", the XBM rate, code 2) is rejected under NLGEOM.

Thermomechanical face

The same composition serves a thermomechanical block (thermomechanical=True in simcoon.solver.solve(), sim.umat_T, the Abaqus umat_singleT) with the name MODUL and the props [rho, c_p] + mat.props: the density and the heat capacity in front of the stream, as the dedicated kernels. The heat of the increment and its derivatives are written once on the mechanisms (ModularUMAT::run_T): the entropy \(\eta = c_0 \ln(\theta/\theta_0) + \boldsymbol{\alpha}:\boldsymbol{\sigma}\), the dissipation \(\Delta\gamma_{loc}\) of the increment, the thermodynamic split of the plastic work (the isotropic hardening work \(\tfrac12 (R_n + R_{n+1})\,\Delta p\) stored in Wm_ir; the back-stress energy \(\sum_i \tfrac12 (\mathbf{X}_i^n + \mathbf{X}_i^{n+1}) : \Delta\mathbf{a}_i\) in Wm_r, since a reverse loading recovers it; the rest dissipated, Wm_d) and \(r = \Delta\gamma_{loc} - \theta_{n+1}\,\Delta\eta\) (see Use the solver). A composition equivalent to EPICP or EPKCP is that kernel, stress, temperature and works to round-off, under every tangent mode and integrator and under both finite-strain forms. The derivatives of the heat are exact for isotropic hardening (the turning of the flow direction is in them) and hold the direction of the back stresses fixed over the increment (Prager as EPKCP, 2e-6 on a non-proportional probe, tested at 1e-5; Armstrong-Frederick and Chaboche 1e-4 under the cutting plane, whose explicit iterates leave the secant \(\Delta\mathbf{a}/\Delta p\) as the derivative of the back-strain, 3e-7 under the closest point, where the backward-Euler update has a consistent one). A viscoelastic mechanism dissipates \(\sum_i \tfrac12 (\mathbf{A}_i^n + \mathbf{A}_i^{n+1}) : \Delta\mathbf{E}^v_i\) on its branch forces \(\mathbf{A}_i = \mathbf{L}_i (\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} - \boldsymbol{\alpha}(\theta - \theta_0) - \mathbf{E}^v_i)\), its heat derivatives exact (linear branches, nothing held fixed): a Maxwell composition is PRONK to round-off, and ZENER (a Kelvin branch in series with the spring) is the one-branch Maxwell composition with, per isotropic mode \(X \in \{K, G\}\), \(X_1^M = X_0^2/(X_0 + X_1)\) and \(\eta^M = \eta^K X_0^2/(X_0 + X_1)^2\) (the map its adapter applies; simcoon.modular.zener_as_maxwell() gives the branch). A damage mechanism dissipates the trapezoid \(\tfrac12 (Y_n + Y_{n+1})\,\Delta D\) on its driving force (exact for the linear law), its heat derivatives written on the effective stress. A hyperelastic block carries its temperature dependence through the omega keyword of the block, \(\Psi = f(\theta)\,\bar{W} + U(J)\) with \(f(\theta) = 1 - \omega + \omega\,\theta/\theta_0\): omega=0 (default) is energetic thermoelasticity, the limit of the linear blocks (the entropy \(c_0 \ln(\theta/\theta_0) + \boldsymbol{\alpha}:\boldsymbol{\sigma}\), cooling under adiabatic stretch); omega=1 the entropic elasticity of a rubber (\(\mu \propto \theta\), the entropy gains \(-\bar{W}/\theta_0\), Gough-Joule heating beyond the thermoelastic inversion at \(\lambda - 1 \simeq \alpha\theta_0\)). The volumetric term stays energetic, the heat capacity at fixed deformation stays \(c_0\), and the mechanical block scales its isochoric stress by \(f(\theta)\) too when the temperature moves (T_init must then be an absolute temperature). “Entropic” names where the elastic energy is stored, not a dissipation: the block is hyperelastic, \(\gamma_{loc} = 0\), its heat \(-\theta\,\Delta\eta\) is reversible (Wt_r) and Wm_ir = Wm_d = 0. A load-unload cycle therefore closes without hysteresis under isothermal and under adiabatic conditions (the heat expelled on loading is taken back on unloading); a loop only opens under a finite heat exchange with the surroundings (thermoelastic damping), where its area is the net heat given to the surroundings and is booked in Wm_r, the closure \(W_m - W_m^{ir} - W_m^d\), which is not a state function when the temperature moves. ncp="min" reaches the face through simcoon.solver.solve() and the ncp keyword of sim.umat_T. The face serves every block and every mechanism, omega != 0 composed with mechanisms included: the entropy then carries \(-\bar{W}(\mathbf{H}_e)/\theta_0\) on the elastic strain \(\mathbf{H}_e = \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} - \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{in} - \boldsymbol{\alpha}\,\Delta\theta\), its derivatives through the chain \(d\mathbf{H}_e = \mathbf{L}^{-1}\,(d\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{eff} - (\omega/\theta_0)\,\mathbf{T}_{iso}\,d\theta)\) on the tangents of the effective stress, \(\mathbf{T}_{iso}\) the isochoric stress of the block at \(f = 1\) (its stress is \(f(\theta)\,\mathbf{T}_{iso} + p_{vol}\mathbf{I}\)) (exact against finite differences with plasticity, viscoelasticity and damage, to the accuracy of those tangents).
